Hi! I am a PhD student in Computer Science at George Mason University, advised by Prof. David Porfirio in the Autonomous Robot Interaction (ARI) Lab. I work on human-robot interaction (HRI), with a focus on how people communicate tasks to autonomous robots.

Before joining the ARI Lab, I worked on device-free human sensing with WiFi signals, including two summers with the IoT research team at JPMorgan Chase, and before my PhD I spent three years at SERVO-ROBOT building machine-vision and machine-learning systems for industrial welding robots. Together these gave me hands-on experience with both real-world robots and sensing people, which I now bring to designing robots that people can work with.

Research

As robots move into everyday environments, people need intuitive ways to tell them what to do. But natural language is often ambiguous, and end-user programs can be overly specific, so what a user says is rarely exactly what they mean. My research focuses on uncovering and refining user intent in human-robot communication. My recent work explores:

  • Eliciting and refining user intent from robot task specifications (DIS'26)
  • End-user robot programming and natural-language interfaces for robots
  • Human-AI communication for autonomous systems
